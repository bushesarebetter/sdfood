Inspect smarter: risk-targeting for San Diego food inspections

Independent analysis by Ayan Pendharkar · SD County DEH already sets inspection frequency by facility category — but a facility's own violation track record barely changes it. This ranks by that.

The evidence (forward-tested, out-of-sample)

Inspect the top 20% by risk → find ~47% of all critical violations (vs 20% by calendar; 2.36× lift). Tested on 36,543 inspections the model never saw.

Critical violations surface ~6 days sooner when a month's scheduled inspections are worked in risk order (95% CI 5.9–6.6), while clean facilities wait under a day longer. This is detection latency within an already-scheduled batch — not prevented illness.

Trained on routine inspections Jan 2023 – Dec 2024; tested on Jan 2025 – Sep 2026 (36,543 inspections the model never saw). All figures above (AUC, gains, days-earlier) come from that held-out test window.

Statistically checked: AUC is stable at 0.73–0.76 across three separate time cutoffs (rolling-origin backtest); the days-earlier estimate carries a bootstrap 95% CI. Full method & code in the repo.

Fairness check — does it target poorer or immigrant neighborhoods?

No — and I checked income and ethnicity. Predicted risk ≈ actual violation rate in every income group (calibrated). Low-income ZIPs are flagged 0.64× as often as high-income ones, because actual violation rates are roughly flat across income (0.96×) — the model tracks real risk, not income.

The one real caveat — equal protection. Because lower-income and higher-Hispanic areas score a bit lower and the model has less signal there, a single global "top-20%" rule catches a smaller share of their real violations (recall 38% vs 54%). That's an under-coverage risk, not over-policing.

Safeguard: keep a baseline of routine/rotating inspections everywhere, monitor recall by group after launch, and treat this as an augment to discretion — not a replacement for mandated frequencies. Full numbers in FAIRNESS.md.

The worklist — who to prioritize next

Every currently-active facility, scored for its next, not-yet-done inspection from its full history to date, then ranked by risk weighted by how overdue it is versus its category's normal cadence (so a high-risk place seen last month doesn't jump one 11 months overdue). This is a prioritization aid within the county's required schedule, not a replacement for it.
